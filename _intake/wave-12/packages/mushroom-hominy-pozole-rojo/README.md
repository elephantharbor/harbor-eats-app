## Steps

### 1. Prep chiles, mushrooms, and garnishes

Bring 2 cups water to a boil in a kettle or small pot. Stem and seed the guajillo and ancho chiles, shaking out the seeds, and tear them flat. Wipe and quarter the mushrooms. Dice half the onion for the pot; finely dice the other half, rinse it under cold water, and drain it for garnish. Peel the garlic; keep 3 cloves whole and mince 2. Finely shred the cabbage, slice the radishes into thin coins, and cut the limes into wedges. Drain and rinse the hominy. HARD CONTRACT: vegetable broth only; tostadas with no lard or cheese; no crema, sour cream, or cheese anywhere on the bowl.

### 2. Toast and soak the chiles

Set a 6-quart Dutch oven over medium heat. Toast the chile pieces in the dry pot, pressing them flat with a spatula, about 15 to 20 seconds per side, until fragrant and lightly blistered. Do not let them blacken; burnt chiles make the broth bitter. Move them to a heatproof bowl, pour the 2 cups boiling water over, and weigh them down with a small plate. Soak until soft, about 15 minutes.

### 3. Brown the mushrooms

While the chiles soak, raise the heat under the same pot to medium-high and add 1 tbsp oil. Add half the mushrooms in a single layer with 1/2 tsp salt. Cook undisturbed 3 minutes, then stir and cook until deeply browned, about 3 minutes more. Move to a plate. Repeat with 1 tbsp oil, the remaining mushrooms, and 1/2 tsp salt.

### 4. Soften the onion and blend the chiles

Lower the heat to medium. Add the last 1 tbsp oil and the diced onion (the pot half) and cook, stirring, until soft and turning golden, about 4 minutes; add the minced garlic for the last minute. While the onion cooks, put the soaked chiles, 1 cup of their soaking liquid, the 3 whole garlic cloves, 1 tsp oregano, the cumin, and 1/2 tsp salt in a blender. Blend on high until completely smooth, about 1 minute. Discard the remaining soaking liquid.

### 5. Fry the chile puree

Pour the puree through a fine-mesh strainer into the pot, pressing the solids with a spoon; discard the skins left in the strainer. Cook, stirring often, until the puree darkens to brick red and thickens, about 4 minutes. This frying step is where the broth gets its depth.

### 6. Simmer the pozole

Add the browned mushrooms and their juices, the hominy, the vegetable broth, and the bay leaves. Bring to a boil over high heat, about 5 minutes, then reduce to a steady simmer. Simmer uncovered until the hominy is plump and tender and the broth tastes rounded, about 15 minutes.

### 7. Season and serve

Remove the bay leaves. Taste and add up to 1/2 tsp salt. Ladle into deep bowls. Top each bowl with shredded cabbage, radish coins, raw diced onion, and a pinch of the remaining oregano crumbled between your palms. Serve with lime wedges to squeeze over and corn tostadas alongside for crunch. No cheese, no crema. kitchen_tested is false.

## Scaling notes

- **1 serving(s):** Blend a half batch of chiles (small chile batches scorch and blend poorly), use 1/4 of it, and freeze the rest. A quarter batch of soup is 6 oz mushrooms, 12 oz hominy, 1 1/2 cups broth, 2 tostadas.
- **2 serving(s):** Half of everything; brown the mushrooms in one batch. If your blender will not puree 3 1/2 chiles smoothly, blend a full batch and freeze the extra puree.
- **4 serving(s):** As written.
- Do not scale: vegetable broth only; no cheese or crema; lard-free tostadas; strain the chile puree; toast chiles briefly, never black
