## Steps

### 1. Prep spinach and paneer

Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the baby spinach and cook just until collapsed, about 1 minute. Drain and rinse under cold water. Squeeze out excess water firmly. Chop the spinach roughly, or pulse a few times in a food processor to a coarse puree (not a baby-food slick). Cut the paneer into 3/4-inch cubes and pat dry. Dice the onion. Mince the garlic, ginger, and jalapeño. Measure the spices, cream, butter, oil, tomatoes, salt, cilantro, and lemon juice.

### 2. Brown the paneer

Heat 2 tbsp of the neutral oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add the paneer cubes and 1/4 tsp of the kosher salt. Cook, turning, until golden on several sides, about 5 to 6 minutes. Transfer the paneer to a plate. Do not add meat. Do not substitute tofu in this package and still call it saag paneer.

### 3. Build the onion-spice base

Reduce heat to medium. Add the remaining 1 tbsp neutral oil. Add the yellow onion and cook until soft and lightly golden, about 6 minutes. Add the garlic, ginger, and jalapeño. Cook 1 minute. Stir in the ground cumin, ground coriander, ground turmeric, 1/2 tsp of the garam masala, and the optional cayenne. Stir 30 to 60 seconds until fragrant.

### 4. Simmer tomatoes and spinach

Add the canned diced tomatoes and 1 tsp of the kosher salt. Cook, stirring, until the tomatoes look jammy and most free liquid has cooked down, about 5 minutes. Stir in the chopped spinach and up to 1/2 cup water as needed so the saag is saucy but not watery. Simmer about 5 minutes. This spinach-tomato saag is made in the skillet, not a jarred spinach sauce.

### 5. Return paneer and finish with dairy

Return the browned paneer to the skillet. Simmer 3 minutes so the cubes heat through. Stir in the heavy cream, unsalted butter, and the remaining 1/2 tsp garam masala. Warm through 1 minute without a hard boil. Take off the heat. Stir in the lemon juice and cilantro. Serve as a skillet saag with paneer cubes visible. No chicken. No lamb. kitchen_tested is false.
