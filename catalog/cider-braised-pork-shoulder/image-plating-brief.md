# Image / Plating Brief — Cider-Braised Pork Shoulder with Mustard Cabbage

Source (read-only): certified package recipe only. `README.md` and `v1.json` `recipe_version`. No recipe text was changed for this brief.

## Hero subject

Braised boneless pork shoulder with spoon-coating cider-mustard sauce and soft apple, parsley on the meat, and skillet mustard cabbage wedges beside it. Pork is the hero. Cabbage is the required side on the same plate, not a garnish.

## Vessel

Dinner plate. The Dutch oven is the braise pot, not the required serving vessel. The recipe says to serve cabbage beside the meat. No bread plate, no potato bowl.

## Camera angle

Slightly elevated 3/4 so sauce, apple, pork surface, and cabbage wedges all read. Avoid a tight overhead that turns the plate into a brown disk.

## Required visible components

- Boneless pork shoulder, one piece, deeply browned from the sear, then braised until fork-tender (195–203°F). After the rest and after any twine is removed: if it holds, 1/2-inch slices against the grain; if it collapses, large chunks. Not a fine shred and not a puree.
- Cider-mustard sauce spooned over the pork: reduced until it coats a spoon (about 1 cup), with the soft apple mashed into it. Onion, smashed garlic, and Dijon are in the sauce. Thyme sprigs are discarded before serving and must not appear.
- Skillet mustard cabbage: a small head cut into 8 wedges through the core so they hold, cut face browned, then finished with water, apple cider vinegar, Dijon, and salt until the core is tender. Wedges beside the meat, not a raw slaw.
- Chopped fresh parsley sprinkled on the pork.

## Texture cues

Pork looks moist and tender, with a deep brown sear, not dry roasted slices and not pulled-pork fibers. Sauce looks glossy and spoon-coating, with soft apple, not a watery broth and not a flour-thickened gravy. Cabbage wedges show a browned cut face and a tender but intact core. Parsley is fresh-cut green.

## Sauce/component placement

Spoon the cider sauce and the mashed soft apple over the pork. Do not paint decorative arcs. Dijon is already in the sauce (2 tbsp) and on the cabbage (1 tbsp); do not add a mustard dollop. Parsley goes on the pork. Cabbage sits beside the meat. Skimmed fat should not leave a grease lake.

## Legitimate garnish (recipe-listed only)

Chopped fresh parsley (2 tbsp) on the pork. No other garnish is listed. Thyme is cooked in the braise and then discarded.

## Environmental styling (recipe-corresponding props only; optional)

Optional and small, off the plate, outside the center crop: a parsley sprig, or one raw apple only if it is clearly a prep prop and not a served raw garnish (the served apple is mashed into the sauce). Do not show a cider glass, a vinegar bottle, or bread. Prefer no apple prop if it could be read as an uncooked side.

## Natural color opportunities

Deep brown pork and sauce, pale gold soft apple, green parsley, green cabbage with a browned face. The dish is naturally low-color. Do not add cranberry, carrots, or herbs that are not in the recipe.

## Forbidden elements (ingredients/sides/techniques NOT in the recipe)

Bread, potatoes, noodles, carrots, cranberries, cream, flour-thickened gravy, barbecue sauce, a raw apple fan, thyme sprigs on the plate, kitchen twine left on the meat, a fine pulled-pork pile, a puree, hard cider or a drinking glass, apple cider vinegar as a visible separate puddle, or a sandwich.

## Crop strategy (1200x900 master, 640x480 card, FlavorWeave recommendation-card crop)

Master 1200×900 and 640×480 card are the same 4:3 frame. Keep pork, sauce, and at least two cabbage wedges inside the central 75% of the width. The mobile recommendation card is a square center cover-crop of the 4:3 frame (about 12.5% off each side); tablet cards stay 4:3. Parsley and the browned cabbage face must remain visible at card scale. Do not shove the cabbage to the outer edge.

## Ambiguity (not resolved; recipe not edited)

The serve step allows two pork shapes: 1/2-inch slices if the shoulder holds, or large chunks if it collapses. It forbids a fine shred and a puree, but it does not say which of the two legal shapes this piece took. That choice changes the image. Do not pick one by editing the recipe. Also, “mash the soft apple into the sauce” can read as visible soft pieces or a fully smooth mash; the recipe does not say how smooth.
