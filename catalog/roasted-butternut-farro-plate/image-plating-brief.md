# Image / Plating Brief — Roasted Butternut Plate with Farro and Lemon-Herb Dressing

Source (read-only): certified package recipe only. `README.md` and `v1.json` `recipe_version`. No recipe text was changed for this brief.

## Hero subject

A composed dinner plate: oven-roasted butternut wedges on one side, simmered pearled farro on the other, lemon-herb dressing spooned across both. Neither component is tossed into a bowl. Squash and farro share the frame. This is not a grain bowl.

## Vessel

Dinner plate, not a bowl. The recipe says this explicitly.

## Camera angle

Slightly elevated 3/4 so the plate reads as two components side by side, with dressing visible on both. A steep overhead is acceptable only if the farro grains and the squash edges stay distinct. Do not use a bowl-overhead composition.

## Required visible components

- Butternut squash, seeded and cut into 1-inch wedges, roasted at 425°F about 35 minutes, flipped at 20, edges browned, knife-tender. No flour and no breading. The recipe does not say to peel the squash. See ambiguity.
- Pearled farro, rinsed, simmered about 30 minutes until tender with some grains split, drained. Grains must be identifiable as farro, not couscous, rice, quinoa, or orzo. The bay leaf is discarded and must not be on the plate.
- Lemon-herb dressing spooned across both sides: olive oil, lemon zest and all of the juice, minced garlic, Dijon, chopped parsley, salt, and pepper, lightly thickened from whisking. Not a pool that drowns the plate.

## Texture cues

Squash wedges with browned edges and a soft face. Farro grains separate, chewy-looking, some split. Dressing looks emulsified and herby, not a clear oil puddle and not a cream sauce. Parsley is chopped into the dressing.

## Sauce/component placement

Do not toss squash and farro together. Farro on one side, wedges on the other. Spoon the dressing across both. Dijon stays inside the dressing, not as a mustard dollop. No cheese crumble, no seed sprinkle, no honey.

## Legitimate garnish (recipe-listed only)

Chopped parsley is part of the dressing, not a separate garnish pile. Lemon zest is in the dressing. No other finish is listed. Do not add a lemon wedge: all zest and juice go into the dressing.

## Environmental styling (recipe-corresponding props only; optional)

Optional and small, outside the center crop: a parsley sprig. Do not set a lemon half next to the plate; the recipe uses the whole lemon in the dressing and the image would imply a wedge that is not served. Do not show a bay leaf, a honey jar, or squash seeds as a topping (seeds are removed when the squash is seeded; they are not a garnish).

## Natural color opportunities

Orange roasted squash with browned edges, tan farro, green parsley in the dressing, yellow of lemon only as zest in the dressing. Do not add pomegranate, nuts, pepitas, or greens that are not parsley.

## Forbidden elements (ingredients/sides/techniques NOT in the recipe)

Cheese, nuts, seeds, pepitas, bread, honey, a lemon wedge, a bay leaf on the plate, couscous or any grain other than pearled farro, a bowl composition, flour, breading, cream, butter, kale or spinach, and tossing the squash into the grain. The recipe says no cheese, no nuts, no seeds, and no bread, and says not to add water, honey, or cheese to the dressing.

## Crop strategy (1200x900 master, 640x480 card, FlavorWeave recommendation-card crop)

Master 1200×900 and 640×480 card are one 4:3 frame. Keep the boundary between farro and squash near the middle, with both components inside the central 75% of the width. The mobile recommendation card square-crops that frame from the center (`object-fit: cover`, about 12.5% off each side); tablet cards stay 4:3. Both farro grains and browned squash must survive the square crop. Do not push either component fully to the outer edge.

## Ambiguity (not resolved; recipe not edited)

The squash step says to seed it and cut 1-inch wedges. It does not say to peel it. Skin-on wedges versus peeled wedges change the image. This brief does not add a peeling step. Do not show peeled cubes; cubes are not the cut the recipe uses.
