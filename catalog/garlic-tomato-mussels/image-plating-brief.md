# Image / Plating Brief — Garlic Tomato Mussels with Grilled Bread

Source (read-only): certified package recipe only. `README.md` and `v1.json` `recipe_version`. No recipe text was changed for this brief. The recipe calls the bread a skillet char, not a separate grilled meal, even though the title says “with Grilled Bread.”

## Hero subject

Open mussels in a garlic and crushed-tomato broth, finished with parsley and lemon, with oil-brushed crusty bread toasted until the edges char. Mussels and broth are the hero. Bread is beside them, not a second course that takes over the frame.

## Vessel

The recipe says to serve the mussels in their broth. It does not say to move them to a bowl. The cooking vessel is a wide 8-quart pot, which is an awkward hero scale. See ambiguity below. Whatever holds the mussels must show shells sitting in tomato broth, not a dry pile of shells and not a pasta bowl.

## Camera angle

Slightly elevated 3/4, high enough to read open shells and red broth, low enough to see broth depth and charred bread edges. Not a tight macro of one mussel.

## Required visible components

- Farmed mussels in the shell, scrubbed, open. The recipe says to discard any that stay closed, so closed shells are not part of the served meal.
- Tomato-garlic broth: olive oil, softened diced yellow onion, sliced garlic that has not browned, a small amount of red pepper flakes, canned crushed tomatoes, dry white wine simmered in, salt and pepper. Broth looks like tomatoes thickened slightly, not a cream soup and not a thin clear steam.
- Chopped fresh parsley tossed in off the heat.
- Lemon: juice of half a lemon in the pot, the other half cut into wedges and served.
- Crusty bread (baguette or small ciabatta), 8 slices in the recipe, brushed with olive oil and toasted in a skillet or grill pan until the edges char (about 1–2 minutes per side). Show several slices, not a whole untoasted loaf as the meal.

## Texture cues

Plump open mussels, not shrunken. Glossy tomato broth. Soft onion, pale sliced garlic. Bread crust with distinct charred edges and an oily sheen. Parsley looks freshly cut, not cooked down.

## Sauce/component placement

Broth surrounds the mussels in the pot. Parsley and the squeezed lemon half are finished in the pot, not painted on as a restaurant swoosh. Lemon wedges and toasted bread are served with the mussels, beside or at the rim, not buried.

## Legitimate garnish (recipe-listed only)

Chopped parsley (1/4 cup) and lemon wedges from the unsqueezed half. Both are ingredient roles marked garnish. Nothing else.

## Environmental styling (recipe-corresponding props only; optional)

Optional and small: a parsley sprig or a lemon wedge already required by the serve step, kept at the edge. Do not add a wine glass, a second loaf, or mussel shells arranged as decoration beyond the served portion. Props stay out of the center crop.

## Natural color opportunities

Orange-red tomato broth, dark shells, green parsley, yellow lemon wedges, charred bread. Red pepper flakes are a speck, not a chili-flake snowfall. Do not add cream, basil, or saffron for color.

## Forbidden elements (ingredients/sides/techniques NOT in the recipe)

Pasta, cheese, cream, butter, fennel, chili oil, a browned garlic fry (the recipe says not to let the garlic brown), french fries, a green salad, a grilled (outdoor-grill) presentation that replaces the skillet bread, closed mussels served as food, raw wine poured at the table, or any protein besides mussels.

## Crop strategy (1200x900 master, 640x480 card, FlavorWeave recommendation-card crop)

Master 1200×900 and 640×480 card share one 4:3 frame. Cluster open mussels and broth in the center. Keep at least one lemon wedge and a charred bread edge inside the central 75% of the width so the square recommendation-card crop (mobile 1:1 `object-fit: cover`, about 12.5% off each side; tablet cards remain 4:3) still shows mussels, red broth, and a hint of bread or lemon. Do not push the bread entirely to the outer edge.

## Ambiguity (not resolved; recipe not edited)

The serve step does not name a serving vessel, only “in their broth,” while the pot is a wide 8-quart. A tight crop of that pot versus a transfer into a wide shallow bowl would change the image. This brief does not pick a transfer the recipe does not describe. Show mussels in the cooking broth; do not invent a plated bowl course.
